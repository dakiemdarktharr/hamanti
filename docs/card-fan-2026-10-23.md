# Card fan and image publication

The index now uses portrait cards with rounded paper edges, an overlapping arc,
and visible SVG-backed decks at the upper right and lower left. Seven main cards
fit desktop, five fit tablet, and mobile retains one readable card and a preview.
The extra small card at the edge is the transfer between a deck and the hand.

Scroll position determines every pose. Cards enter from the upper deck, curl
onto the right of the fan, pass over its centre and curl into the lower deck.
Reverse scrolling retraces the path. Cubic joins preserve the position and
velocity where each transfer meets the main arc. The transfer endpoints match
the visible decks exactly. Both decks and the cards follow the same horizontal
scroll surface as the shan shui landscape.

The presentation uses CSS transforms, without adding 3D models, renderers or
dependencies. Updates touch only nearby cards and two deck elements. Hover and
keyboard focus raise the selected card above its neighbours. A single search
result is centred without decorative decks; no-result and first-letter filters
retain their existing controls. Reduced motion disables smoothing and hover
movement while retaining manual navigation.

A bounded, lightly damped spring adds a small torsion and vertical give while
scrolling, with a brief rebound as the cards settle. Its exact damped solution
keeps the response consistent across refresh rates. At rest it stops scheduling
frames. It is disabled for reduced motion and immediate navigation jumps.
Landscape generation and painting now both happen inside the worker. A bounded
OffscreenCanvas paints the generator's SVG paths and washes, then transfers the
finished bitmap to the page. Software paint avoids blocking the UI's shared GPU
queue with thousands of paths. No new dependency or 3D renderer is required.
Larger viewport changes regenerate at the appropriate density. Older browsers
retain the SVG fallback; its decoding waits until motion finishes, and native
scroll events no longer overwrite an explicit landscape hold. Initial index/about
deep links are restored after the opening layout is measured, under the loader.
Before a new gesture or navigation, a stale Lenis position is synchronized with
the visible native position. This prevents the first wheel gesture after an
index deep link from jumping back toward the title.

An eight-second Chromium frame sample at 1440×900 included two new landscape
panels and forward/reverse scrolling: 1,365 frames, 6.1 ms at the 95th percentile,
22.2 ms maximum, and no long tasks. This replaces the measured 429 ms main-thread
SVG swap task; timing is specific to the local browser and machine. The two
bitmap swaps each took a 5.5 ms frame interval. A pixel comparison of translated
paths, overlapping washes, open strokes, circles and rotated text against native
SVG had a mean channel difference of 0.053/255 (antialiasing differences). The
SVG fallback, mobile-to-desktop density change, search/detail/home flow and
system reduced motion were checked separately without page errors. All 25
automated tests pass, including spring response at 60 and 120 Hz and settling.

Browser checks covered 1920×1080, 1440×900, 1024×900, 390×844 and 1280×560
layouts; wheel movement in both directions; reversible intermediate poses;
search, clear and empty results; the Z filter; keyboard card selection; footer
navigation; system reduced motion; Acidgrafix's ten-image gallery; and viewer
navigation and Escape. A frame sample of the home transition confirmed that
the detail page remained present throughout the closing wipe. No page errors
were recorded in that interaction check.

The MongoDB import completed with 157 approved images across 23 styles,
including 95 newly eligible originals and all ten Acidgrafix files. It read back
stored bytes and dimensions with no import failures. The intake directory is
preserved. Resolution and visual review decisions are documented in
`folder-publication-2026-10-21.md` and the portable data manifests.
